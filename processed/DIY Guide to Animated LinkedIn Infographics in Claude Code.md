### TL;DR:

→ **Psychology before tactics.** People come to LinkedIn to learn, but they still want to be entertained, and they want to feel smart. A visual hook buys you the first two seconds. Tim’s lead-cleaning GIF shows water being filtered, which is oddly satisfying to watch and tells you what the process does before you read a word.

→ **Design for saves and reposts.** The algorithm currently over-indexes on saves, so a step-by-step breakdown with a “save this” call to action works well. Nobody implements a process while scrolling. Reposts come from a different motive. One of his posts got 27 reposts, because sharing a sophisticated graphic makes you look smart in front of your network.

→ **In B2B, look complex and read simple.** Tim’s working theory is that B2C rewards the simplest possible visual and B2B is the inverse. Dense on the outside signals value. Each step inside should still be dead simple. Watch the line where dense turns illegible.

→ **Copy first, animation last.** Start with something true from your own experience, then build the static graphic, and only then animate it.

→ **The repo does the heavy lifting.** Claude file with the brand rules. A detailed profile of who he is, his results and hundreds of past posts for voice. A brand folder with fonts, icons, tokens, and coded components. An ideal-follower file that covers psychographics, not only firmographics. Then the skills on top. It took him about nine months and four iterations, and it is why his deliberately sloppy live prompt still came out on brand. You can copy the structure. You still have to spend some time talking to Claude about who you are.

→ **The animation is just code.** HTML for structure, CSS for the brand, JavaScript for motion, rendered to GIF. Claude installs and runs all the tools it needs. The skill makes Claude review and improve its own output in a loop, with Tim as the final reviewer. He usually needs two or three rounds.

→ **Content is half the system.** Tim batches five posts on a Sunday and turns one YouTube idea into LinkedIn posts, X posts and a newsletter, with a skill for each step. Then he pulls his post engagers, qualifies them against his ICP and starts a conversation (no pitch-slapping).

Thanks to Tim for opening up his whole setup.
