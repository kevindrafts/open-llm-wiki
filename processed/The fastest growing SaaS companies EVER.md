## How Cursor became the fastest-growing SaaS in history

Unlike other SaaS rocket ships that scaled through big-ticket enterprise contracts, Cursor’s growth playbook is fundamentally different:

Cursor flipped the traditional SaaS model on its head.

Instead of chasing big whales, they went after a massive school of tiny fish — and it worked.

## The elephant in the room

Let’s be real — AI is the hottest thing in tech right now.

The insane hype cycle around AI-powered tools definitely accelerated Cursor’s growth.

Every developer wants an AI coding assistant.

AI-first startups are getting massive funding rounds.

Companies are adopting AI tools like wildfire, fearing they’ll be left behind.

Cursor rode this wave perfectly.

Would it have grown without AI? Probably.

Would it have hit $100M ARR in 12 months without AI hype? Doubtful.

As I always say: great markets make for great marketing.

But while AI hype was a major accelerant, it wasn’t the only reason Cursor grew fast.

---

## Why forking VS Code was a genius move

One of the biggest reasons Cursor grew so fast (besides AI) is because they forked VS Code.

Why does this matter? Because it made switching costs nearly zero.

VS Code is the most popular IDE in the world.

Millions of developers already use it daily.

It has a massive, mature extension marketplace.

By forking VS Code, Cursor removed friction from the adoption process:

No need to learn a new interface — Cursor feels exactly like VS Code.

No disruption to developer workflows — same extensions, same file handling, same keyboard shortcuts.

No risk in trying it — developers could test Cursor without any real switching pain.

This was brilliant because developers hate changing their IDEs. The less friction, the better.

💡 Key takeaway: Lower switching costs = higher adoption rates.

Cursor didn’t just make a great product — they made it effortless to switch.

## The 4 growth levers that drove Cursor’s insane scale

## 1\. They piggybacked on an existing ecosystem

Cursor sidestepped the hardest part of growth: distribution.

They didn’t try to convince developers to adopt a brand-new coding environment.

Instead, they built on top of what devs were already using (VS Code).

💡 Key takeaway: Instead of trying to create demand from scratch, embed yourself into an existing ecosystem.

## 2\. They nailed the PLG model (Product-Led Growth)

Most hyper-growth SaaS companies scale through enterprise sales.

Cursor skipped that completely — zero friction, zero sales team, zero long cycles.

💡 Key takeaway: The best PLG models reduce friction to nearly zero. Cursor made it effortless for developers to start using it.

## 3\. They monetized the “middle” of the market

Cursor found a third option — the middle of the market.

By stacking 360,000+ individual users, Cursor outpaced high-ticket SaaS in total revenue — without the headaches of sales cycles.

💡 Key takeaway: You don’t have to go enterprise to scale to $100M ARR. If your market is big enough, a high-volume, mid-ticket model can work just as well.

## 4\. AI + Developer Tools = perfect timing

Cursor launched at the exact right moment, when:

AI-powered workflows were exploding

Developers were actively searching for AI coding assistants

Companies were increasing AI budgets across the board

💡 Key takeaway: Timing matters. Cursor launched at the perfect moment when AI dev tools were booming.

## Final thoughts

Cursor rewrote the SaaS playbook with:

AI timing + PLG + low switching costs = rapid adoption

Built on an existing habit (VS Code) to make switching frictionless

Monetized a massive user base instead of chasing enterprise deals

If you’re building SaaS today, ask yourself:

Can I piggyback on an existing ecosystem instead of starting from scratch?

How can I make switching costs close to zero?

Am I launching at the right time, or am I too early/late to the trend?
