B2B sales frameworks help teams decide which leads to work, earn trust, improve the buying experience, involve executives, and align compensation with expected behavior. GTMnow's October 1, 2026 roundup connects these decisions through five practitioner accounts. For small teams, the useful starting point is a specific gap in the sales process and a measurable outcome, with AI assigned a clear role and humans handling qualified conversations.

# B2B sales process and GTM frameworks

## Recover leads that nobody works

Salesforce's Kris Billmaier describes an engagement agent assigned to low-scored inbound leads that sellers did not contact. The newsletter says scores 1 and 2 represented about 75% of lead volume, while humans worked scores 3 through 5. The process is to measure the untouched pool, restrict the agent to it, qualify responses, and hand engaged prospects to sellers.

The account reports more than $100 million in pipeline in about eight months and compares agent output to roughly 400 additional reps alongside about 100 human reps. These are reported pipeline and capacity claims, not independently verified revenue or evidence of equivalent work across roles. The transferable measure is incremental qualified pipeline from the previously untouched pool. See [[ai-marketing-automation-workflows]] and [[decision-models-for-gtm]].

## Earn trust through relevant evidence

LinkedIn's Catherine Flynn emphasizes timely outreach after a job change, company announcement, product launch, or other buying signal. Start with the buyer's problem and a useful point of view. Build relationships across the buying group through customer advocates, former customers at new employers, mutual connections, and executive sponsors.

The clipping cites 86% of buyers valuing expertise and 45% describing the sellers they meet as trustworthy. Its surrounding discussion also calls this a seller-versus-buyer trust gap, but those two percentages alone do not measure sellers' self-assessment. It reports nearly three times the InMail response through mutual connections and greater executive-sponsor use among top sellers, without enough methodology to infer causation. The practical connection to [[proof-of-work-and-side-door-outreach]] is to make relevance visible before asking for time.

## Improve the experience buyers actually have

Deel's Chris Lee maps the buyer's experience from first contact to signature, rates each step, and imagines progressively better versions. This uses buyer-visible moments rather than internal CRM stages. The source's examples include flexible meeting times, leadership introductions after demos, direct access for feedback, and tracking prospect sentiment about the sales process.

A small team's adaptation is to identify one weak moment, such as an unclear demo follow-up or repeated discovery questions, improve it, and review buyer feedback. This is a wiki application of the framework. The article does not establish that every buyer needs executive introductions or unusually flexible availability.

## Keep executives informed without demanding a meeting

Accord's Ross Rich describes a short executive note after a substantive discovery conversation with RevOps, enablement, or a sales leader. A similarly senior person at the seller's company sends it, summarizes the buyer's priority, acknowledges work with the team, and offers brief asynchronous updates. Executive involvement grows as the evaluation matures.

Despite the name "no-ask note," the source's example asks whether anyone else should be involved. The distinction is avoiding an early meeting request while giving the executive context and a chance to guide stakeholder coverage. The note needs a real discovery conversation behind it.

## Align compensation inputs

Notion's Brian Le describes three inputs that need to agree:

- Pay mix should reflect how much control a seller has over the outcome.
- Quotas and payout curves should reconcile the company plan with actual selling capacity and historical conversion.
- Governance should make crediting, leave, and holdover rules clear and dependable.

Quarter-end deal bunching and almost everyone exceeding quota are signals to investigate plan assumptions. They are not proof of a compensation defect by themselves. The clipping reports growth from about 80 to 90 commissioned staff to more than 400 in roughly 2.5 years, so its operating context differs from a solo founder's.

The news roundup also describes Ascerta tracking AI cost, usage, and business outcomes; Listen Labs supporting repeated customer interviews; Modulate analyzing voice calls; EliseAI handling apartment operations; and Row Zero bringing large spreadsheets into Databricks. These are dated newsletter descriptions, including acquisition and funding claims, not independently verified company profiles. The reusable business patterns are measuring automation economics, continuous customer research, and tools built around a specific operating workflow.
