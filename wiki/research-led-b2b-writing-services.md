Research-led B2B writing is a freelance service that turns firsthand software testing into comparisons, tutorials, and other content for software companies. Chris Koerner's September 24, 2026 newsletter profiles Steph, an Austin writer who reportedly charges $900-$1,200 per article. The useful business pattern combines a narrow specialty, samples that demonstrate the work, tailored client outreach, and AI drafting from the writer's own research.

# Research-led B2B writing services

## Offer and evidence

The newsletter describes assignments of roughly 2,500-3,000 words for companies such as Zapier, Jotform, and Softr. Examples include software comparisons, alternatives lists, and workflow tutorials. Clients typically supply target keywords. The writer tests the products, captures screenshots, checks confusing details such as pricing, and uses user discussions to identify questions worth investigating.

Koerner reports that Steph earns around $6,000-$7,000 monthly, mostly from writing, and spends five to six hours per article. These are reported results for a writer who started on Upwork in 2016, not evidence of typical beginner earnings. The clipping's claim that searches for hiring writers reached a five-year high does not include the underlying data. It also does not demonstrate that these articles improve rankings or AI-search visibility.

## Finding and retaining clients

The source proposes a practical sequence:

1. Choose a writing specialty and an industry you already have some interest in.
2. Build four or five relevant samples before seeking paid assignments. Software comparisons based on tools you have used can demonstrate both research and writing ability.
3. Apply selectively on Upwork. Steph reportedly applies to about ten jobs daily when seeking work, favoring assignments with a path to recurring contracts.
4. Send short pitches that reference something specific about the company and link to relevant samples. The newsletter describes successful pitches based on trying a founder's product and listening to an agency's podcast.
5. Expand trusted accounts through article series, publishing support, or landing-page work.

The samples and company-specific pitches connect to [[proof-of-work-and-side-door-outreach]]. This service can start with direct client acquisition, without first building the audience described in [[creator-led-solopreneurship]].

## Scope and pricing

The source suggests $30-$40 hourly for early work, followed by flat project quotes as experience grows. Treat that range as the author's guidance, not a verified market benchmark. Before quoting, clarify length, existing copy, research needs, deadlines, and deliverables. A practical addition is to specify revision limits and whether uploading the article is included.

At $1,000 per article and five to six production hours, gross revenue per production hour is about $167-$200. Five articles yield $5,000; ten yield $10,000 and require roughly 50-60 production hours. Those calculations exclude prospecting, calls, administration, unpaid revisions, platform fees, tools, and taxes. They do not establish an effective hourly take-home rate or a full monthly workload.

## AI workflow built on firsthand work

Steph describes recording her screen and narrating while building a workflow, then asking AI to turn that recording into a draft using her template. She reports using Gemini for audio and video, Claude for marketing content, and ChatGPT for research. These are her stated tool choices, not a comparative evaluation.

The reusable workflow is to test the software, retain evidence, capture the explanation, draft from those inputs, and edit against the actual steps. Steph estimates that she writes 70%-80% of the final words. The source also mentions an approximately 18% AI-workflow figure from a report she edited, but supplies too little methodology to use it as a general benchmark.

This adds a concrete input method to [[ai-marketing-automation-workflows]] and the firsthand-evidence emphasis in [[seo-and-ai-search-strategy]]. The buyer pays for accurate product experience and a useful explanation; faster drafting only helps if those survive editing.

## Ideas to test

For a builder who already experiments with AI tools, a small portfolio of documented comparisons and workflow tutorials could test demand for a writing service. A bounded initial offer might include one tested workflow, screenshots, an article, and a defined revision round. Client interest and repeat assignments would validate the offer; the newsletter's reported income alone does not.
