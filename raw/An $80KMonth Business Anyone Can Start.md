### What He Does:

He “installs” whole-home water systems. A softener that pulls the hardness out of your water, a carbon filter that strips the chlorine and other junk, and a small reverse osmosis unit under the sink for drinking water. He bundles all three and charges $2,699, fully installed, tax included.

But Parker is not a plumber. He never touches an install. **He partners with independent licensed plumbers who do the physical work, and he keeps the sales and marketing for himself.** The plumbers own the job, and Parker owns the phone.

That's the business strategy: **He runs an ad, closes the customer, and hands the job to a plumber who shows up and installs it in about four hours.**

### The Numbers

***Each $2,699 job breaks down like this:***  
\-Roughly $950 in hardware.  
\-About $100 in miscellaneous parts.  
\-Around $500 to the plumber.  
\-That leaves close to $1,000 in gross profit on every single job.  
\-Gross margins land near 40%, and **net margins sit around 30% after ad spend.**

June was his first month, and he did about $15,000 in sales across five installs. July jumped to just under $50,000 on sixteen jobs. August hit $81,000 in revenue. By the first week of September he already had $54,000 booked on the calendar, and all of it came from one channel.

His cash flow works in his favor. He keeps inventory on hand and installs fast, so he collects from the customer quickly and pays his plumbers and suppliers on his own terms. **That's how you start a business like this with almost no money.**

### Why This Works:

The billion-dollar companies (Culligan, Kinetico, Leaf Home, etc) sell a comparable system for $6,000 to $8,000. They send a rep to your house, test your water, and run a long in-person pitch. Plenty of people happily pay that. But plenty more get priced out and get nothing.

Parker basically opened a Chevy dealership right next to the Ferrari dealership. Same destination, way cheaper ride. His best-performing ad was a plain static image of the unit that just said " *Whole home plus drinking water, $2,699 installed.*" **He closed his very first deal on $41 of ad spend.**

(He isn't trying to necessarily educate anyone. He wants the customer who already got quoted $8,000 last month and just needs to hear that a cheaper option exists.)

### How To Start This In Your Area

1. **Build the tech stack.** Parker used Claude to build his website, GoHighLevel for his CRM and phone line, Jobber for scheduling, and Google Workspace for email. That runs about $300 a month. Add a general liability plumbing policy for roughly $180 a month. Total setup was around $500.
2. **Find your plumbers first.** This is vital. Parker posted a sponsored job on Indeed, spent $90 a day for four days, got four qualified applicants, and hired three. He pays $75 to $125 an hour. He targets the independent guy doing good work about 30 hours a week who would love to be at 40 or 50. Take him to coffee. Be honest that you're new and a fast learner.
3. **Run Facebook ads.** Static images beat video for him every time, and he makes them with ChatGPT's image tool. He uses broad geo targeting, homeowners aged 30 to 60, a radius around his city. He optimizes for leads with an instant form that dumps straight into his CRM. His cost per lead runs $30 to $40. Put the price in the ad and let the algorithm find the people who already know what this stuff costs.
4. **Follow up like a maniac.** Only one of his last eighteen closed customers bought on the day they opted in. The rest took multiple touch points, and his longest lead-to-close was 49 days with more than twenty touches. Text first, then call. Never quit on a lead until they tell you to stop. When you do the math, quitting makes no sense. A dead phone call costs you sixty seconds. Closing that customer makes you a thousand bucks.
5. **Beg for reviews.** Parker collected 52 five-star Google reviews in under 85 days. After every install he swings by with free softener salt and asks the customer, their spouse, and their neighbor to leave one. On the east side of his city he's now the number one ranked water filtration company on Google.

### The Lesson

There is a shrinking pool of skilled tradespeople in this country. *Way* more are retiring than starting. That means dozens of high-ticket services have a going rate that a handful of local companies basically made up. Parker's example is an $8,000 water system that costs him $1,500 to deliver.

Water filtration is just the product he happened to pick. Main panel upgrades, water heater installs, and a dozen other trade services have the exact same gap sitting wide open.

**You don't have to be good at the trade itself.** You just need a website, a clean ad with a price on it, a decent sales script, and enough knowledge to talk to an installer without sounding lost.

### Start This Week:

Pick a high-ticket home service in your area. (Doesn’t have to be water filtration) Look up what the big players charge for it. Then figure out what it actually costs to deliver, and wedge the market on price. Post a plumber or electrician or installer job on Indeed. Build a one-page website. Launch one static ad with the price right on the image.

Parker went from DoorDash to $80,000 a month in 85 days because he moved fast and refused to overthink it. You can run the same playbook in your own town, starting today.
