## 1\. Businesses don't buy AI. They buy outcomes

So, how do you know if an AI opportunity is actually worth pursuing?

Corey gave a simple test: **it should improve effectiveness, efficiency, or quality.**

| **Outcome** | **Ask** | **Look for** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Effectiveness** | Can this help the business make more money? | More sales, higher conversion, increased capacity |
| **Efficiency** | Can this give meaningful time back? | Less manual work, faster processes, fewer repetitive tasks |
| **Quality** | Can this make the work better? | Fewer errors, greater consistency, better customer or employee experience |

The important part is making that outcome **measurable**.

Say a team spends five hours every week preparing the same report. Don't stop at *“we can automate this.”*

Do the math: **5 hours/week × 52 weeks = 260 hours/year.**

Now, the opportunity isn't an “AI reporting workflow.” It's whether you can reclaim a meaningful portion of those 260 hours without sacrificing the quality of the work.

A useful way to think about any opportunity is: **Current process → Cost of the problem → Desired outcome → Possible solution**

The AI comes last.

Sometimes the solution is an agent. Sometimes it's an existing tool. Sometimes it's simply fixing the process.

**The takeaway:** Don't go looking for places to use AI. Look for expensive problems where improving effectiveness, efficiency, or quality creates measurable value.

But, how do you find those problems inside a business in the first place?

That's where the assessment becomes valuable.

## 2\. The assessment becomes the front-door offer

Once you've found the right outcome, you still need to understand what's actually happening inside the business before prescribing a solution.

That's where the **AI Assessment** comes in.

Instead of trying to sell an expensive implementation upfront, the assessment turns diagnosis into the first paid engagement.

Corey tested this offer at different price points before landing at **$999**, and the premium course expands the model into a **$999–$1,999 assessment offer**.

The important shift is: **Don't give away discovery so you can sell the build. Make the diagnosis valuable enough to be the first product.**

That gives the client a lower-risk starting point and gives you much better information before anything gets built.

## 3\. Don't automate a broken process

Finding a bottleneck doesn't mean you should immediately automate it.

Corey calls his approach **AOA: Audit → Optimize → Automate.**

| **Step** | **The idea** |
| --- | --- |
| **Audit** | Understand what's actually happening |
| **Optimize** | Fix and simplify the process |
| **Automate** | Only then decide what AI should handle |

The example is simple: if a process has 15 steps but only needs nine, automating all 15 just makes an inefficient process run faster.

**Fix the process first. Automate what remains.**

That's also why selling outcomes matters. If you're only trying to sell an automation, you're incentivized to automate something. If you're trying to improve the outcome, sometimes the right answer is removing the work entirely.

## 4\. The value is in the before and after

If AI helps you finish something in two hours instead of two days, should you charge less?

That's the problem with hourly pricing. The better you get, the faster you deliver, and the less you make.

One example from the session makes this clear: a fintech CEO spent around **two hours every week** writing a company memo. After changing the workflow, that dropped to roughly **15 minutes**.

| **Don't ask** | **Ask instead** |
| --- | --- |
| How long will this take me? | How much time will this save them? |
| What's my hourly rate? | What is that saved time worth? |
| How complex is the build? | How often does this problem occur? |

The value isn't in how sophisticated the AI is. It's in the size of the problem you solve and how often that value repeats.

**The takeaway:** Price the outcome, not the hours it took you to create it.

## 5\. The assessment can lead to recurring revenue

One-off AI projects have an obvious problem: **every finished project means you need to sell another one.**

The alternative Corey shared is the **AI Concierge** model.

Instead of selling another automation every month, the relationship becomes continuous improvement:

**Find a bottleneck → Fix it → Measure the outcome → Find the next one**

That's important because the assessment rarely uncovers just one opportunity. And once a business starts improving its workflows, new bottlenecks become easier to spot.

That's how a $999 assessment can eventually lead to a **$1,000–$2,000/month recurring relationship**, without constantly hunting for another standalone build.

**The takeaway:** Don't make the automation the recurring product. Make continuously finding and solving valuable problems the recurring product.

## 6\. Your first client is probably closer than you think

You don't need a website, a huge audience, or another three months learning AI before testing this.

The 30-day roadmap Corey shared starts much smaller: **make a list of 10 business owners you already know and start there.**

But don't lead with *“I sell AI services.”*

Lead with the problem you can help uncover and the outcome you can create. Reduce the risk of that first engagement, deliver the assessment well, and let the results open the conversation about what comes next.

The progression is simple:

**10 conversations → First assessment → First outcome → Next engagement**

You don't need the entire business figured out before you start. You need enough conversations to find out whether someone will pay for the problem you can solve.

**The takeaway:** Validate the offer with people you already know before spending months building a business around it.
